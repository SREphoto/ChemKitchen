# Centrifuge - Chemistry Lab Operation

## Purpose
To separate components of a mixture based on density differences by applying centrifugal force. Used for separating solids from liquids, immiscible liquids, precipitates, and biological components.

## How It Works
A rotor spins sample tubes at high speed, generating centrifugal force (measured as RCF = relative centrifugal force, or g-force) many times stronger than gravity. Denser particles sediment toward the bottom faster than less dense components. Sedimentation rate depends on particle size, density difference, viscosity, and centrifugal field strength.

## Setup
- Centrifuge machine on stable, level surface
- Select rotor type (fixed-angle, swing-bucket, or vertical) for application
- Balance tubes: opposing tubes must have equal mass (within 0.1g) for safe operation
- Load tubes into rotor symmetrically
- Close and latch rotor lid and centrifuge lid
- Set speed (RPM) and time according to protocol
- For refrigerated centrifuge: set temperature

## Usage
1. Prepare samples in appropriate tubes (conical, microcentrifuge, or thick-walled)
2. Balance tubes exactly - add buffer or water to match masses
3. Load tubes into rotor in balanced positions (opposite each other)
4. Close lid securely (centrifuge will not start if lid is open or unlatched)
5. Set speed (RPM or RCF g-force) and run time
6. Start; do not leave until full speed is reached (listen for unusual noises)
7. After run completes and rotor stops completely: open lid, remove tubes
8. Collect supernatant (pipette) or pellet (resuspend) as needed

## Results
Separated components: supernatant (top) and pellet (bottom) for solid-liquid separation; clear phase separation for immiscible liquids.

## Completion
When timer reaches zero and rotor has completely stopped (do not attempt to stop rotor manually).

- Centrifuge housing: stainless steel, painted steel
- Tubes: polypropylene, polycarbonate, polyethylene, glass (for low speed only)
- Seals/gaskets: rubber, PTFE
- Door: polycarbonate (transparent, shatter-resistant)

## Models
- Microcentrifuge: small tubes (0.5-2 mL), up to 15,000 RPM (21,000 g). Common for DNA/RNA work.
- General purpose: tubes 5-50 mL, up to 5,000-6,000 RPM (5,000 g). For pelleting cells, precipitates.
- High-speed: tubes up to 50-500 mL, up to 20,000-25,000 RPM (50,000 g). Pelleting bacteria, organelles.
- Ultracentrifuge: up to 100,000 RPM (1,000,000 g). Separating viruses, macromolecules, lipoproteins.
- Refrigerated: maintains 2-8°C or lower. For temperature-sensitive samples.
- Cytocentrifuge: specialized for depositing cells onto slides.

## Ventilation
Heat dissipation: centrifuges generate heat, especially at high speeds and during refrigeration. Ensure adequate clearance around vents (typically 6 inches/15 cm per manufacturer spec).

## Limitations
- Maximum speed limited by rotor type and tube material
- Tubes must be properly filled (not overfilled or underfilled)
- Cannot centrifuge flammable or explosive materials (spark risk)
- Some rotors cannot be autoclaved
- Rotors have finite lifetime (fatigue - track number of runs)
- Balanced loading is critical - imbalance can destroy rotor and centrifuge

## Cleaning
- Wipe rotor and chamber after each use with mild detergent or 70% ethanol
- Do not use harsh abrasives - they damage rotor coating
- Clean spills immediately (especially acids, alkalis, radioactive materials)
- Rotor autoclaving: follow manufacturer specs for temperature and duration
- Lubricate O-rings and seals as recommended by manufacturer

## Storage
- Store rotors inverted to drain any residual liquid
- Store in dry environment to prevent corrosion
- Do not store rotors on their drive shaft (store on side or in stand)
- Remove and store rotors separately if centrifuge not in use for extended period

## Disposal
- Centrifuge: dispose as electronic waste per institutional guidelines
- Rotors: metal recycling after decontamination (aluminum or titanium)
- Used tubes: dispose as appropriate chemical/biological waste

## Lab Usage
Used extensively in biochemistry (cell separation, DNA/RNA precipitation), clinical labs (blood separation), analytical chemistry (sample preparation), and industrial chemistry (particle size separation).

## Common Mistakes
- Not balancing tubes properly (causes vibration, rotor damage, injury)
- Using wrong tube type for speed (glass tubes shatter in high-speed centrifuges)
- Overfilling tubes (causes leakage, imbalance)
- Using a rotor past its certified lifetime
- Not securing rotor lid properly (lid can fly off during run)
- Opening lid before rotor stops (balancing safety interlock risk)
- Mixing different tube types on the same rotor

## Common Problems
- Excessive vibration (imbalance, worn rotor, loose rotor)
- Centrifuge won't start (lid not properly closed, rotor not detected, electronic error)
- Brake failure (rotor takes too long to stop)
- Refrigeration not cooling (compressor issue, blocked ventilation)
- Corrosion (acid/base spills not cleaned promptly)
- Rotor cannot be removed (seized on drive shaft)

## Fixes
- Vibration: rebalance tubes, check rotor loading, inspect for loose parts
- Won't start: check lid latch, check rotor detection system, power cycle
- Brake failure: contact service technician; do not manually brake rotor
- Not cooling: clean ventilation filters, check refrigerant, contact service
- Corrosion: clean with appropriate agent, may need rotor replacement if pitted
- Seized rotor: apply manufacturer-approved lubricant, contact service technician

## Safety
- Always balance tubes to within 0.1g or as specified by manufacturer
- Never centrifuge flammable, explosive, or reactive materials unless using specialized explosion-proof equipment
- Never exceed maximum speed rating for rotor and tube combination
- Inspect rotors regularly for cracks, corrosion, or wear
- Do not open centrifuge while rotor is moving (safety interlock prevents this on most models)
- In case of rotor failure (crash): immediately stop centrifuge, contain biohazard if applicable
- Use proper tube caps to prevent leakage
- Register and track rotor usage for lifetime management
- Wear safety glasses when operating
- Have liability insurance for lab operations involving high-speed centrifuges
