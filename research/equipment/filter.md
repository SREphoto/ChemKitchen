# Filter - Chemistry Lab Operation

## Purpose
To separate solid particles from a liquid or gas by passing the mixture through a porous medium (filter). Filtration is used for purification, clarification, sample preparation, and product isolation.

## How It Works
The mixture is passed through a filter medium (filter paper, membrane, fritted glass, or sintered metal) with pores sized to retain particles above a certain size threshold. Gravity, vacuum (suction), pressure, or centrifugal force drives the liquid through the filter while solids collect on the filter surface (cake filtration) or within the filter depth (depth filtration).

## Setup
- Gravity filtration: filter funnel (glass or plastic), filter paper, receiving flask or beaker, support stand with ring clamp
- Vacuum filtration: Büchner funnel or Hirsch funnel, filter paper, filter flask (side-arm), rubber adapter (büchner funnel adapter), vacuum source (water aspirator or vacuum pump), trap flask to prevent backflow
- Membrane filtration: filter holder, membrane filter (0.22-0.45 µm typical), syringe or pressure vessel
- Filter paper selection based on particle retention (coarse, medium, fine) and chemical compatibility

## Usage
1. Select appropriate filter type and pore size for the application
2. For gravity: fold filter paper (fluted for faster flow), place in funnel, wet with solvent, pour mixture through stem gently
3. For vacuum: place filter paper in Büchner funnel, wet and seal to funnel plate, turn on vacuum, pour mixture onto filter, wash cake with solvent while vacuum is on
4. For membrane: attach to syringe or filtration apparatus, pass sample through
5. Collect filtrate (liquid) or retain solids (cake) as needed
6. Air-dry or oven-dry filter cake if solid product is desired

## Results
Clear filtrate (liquid free of visible particles) or isolated solid product. Filtration efficiency determined by clarity of filtrate and completeness of solid recovery.

## Completion
Filtration is complete when no more liquid passes through the filter and the filter cake appears dry (for vacuum filtration). For gravity: when dripping slows to a stop.

## Materials
- Filter paper: cellulose (qualitative and quantitative grades)
- Membrane filters: cellulose acetate, PTFE, polyethersulfone (PES), nylon, PVDF
- Fritted glass: sintered borosilicate glass
- Filtration funnels: borosilicate glass, polypropylene, PTFE
- Filter flasks: borosilicate glass

## Power
- Gravity filtration: no power required
- Vacuum filtration: vacuum pump 100-500W (or water aspirator ‡ no power)
- Pressure filtration: compressed air/gas source or pump
- Membrane filtration: can be manual (syringe) or powered

## Models
- Gravity filtration: simplest, slowest, no special equipment beyond funnel and paper
- Vacuum filtration: faster, uses suction, good for larger quantities, cake drying
- Pressure filtration: for viscous liquids or clogging-prone mixtures
- Membrane filtration: precise pore size cutoff (0.02-10 µm), sterile filtration
- Centrifugal filtration: uses centrifuge to force liquid through filter
- Cross-flow filtration: flow across membrane surface to prevent clogging

## Manuals
Available from Whatman (filtration handbook), Millipore (membrane filtration guide). Product-specific manuals from manufacturers.

## Display / Memory / USB / Connectivity / Voice / Buttons
Manual filtration: none. Automated filtration systems may have digital displays showing pressure, flow rate, and volume. Some have USB for data export. Basic lab filtration is typically manual with no electronics.

## Ventilation
Generally not required for aqueous filtration. For volatile or toxic solvent filtration, use fume hood to prevent vapor exposure.

## Limitations
- Filter can clog with fine particles or high solid loading
- Filter paper may not be compatible with strong acids/bases
- Vacuum filtration limited by vacuum level (~0.1-1 atm differential)
- Cannot filter submicron particles without membrane filters
- Very slow for viscous liquids
- Filter paper can tear under high vacuum

## Cleaning
- Büchner funnel: rinse thoroughly, scrub with brush if needed, wash with detergent
- Sintered glass filters: back-flush with solvent; for stubborn clogs: soak in appropriate cleaning solution (piranha or aqua regia with extreme caution)
- Filter flasks: wash with detergent and brush, rinse with distilled water
- Reusable filter holders: disassemble, clean all parts, rinse, dry

## Storage
- Filter paper: store in sealed packages, dry environment, away from chemical fumes
- Büchner funnels: store clean and dry, protect ground glass joints
- Membrane filters: store in sealed packages, protect from light and heat
- Sintered glass filters: store carefully to prevent breakage

## Disposal
- Used filter paper with chemical residues: dispose as hazardous solid waste
- Membrane filters: dispose according to waste type (biological, chemical)
- Broken glass filtration equipment: dispose as laboratory glass waste

## Lab Usage
One of the most common lab operations. Used in purification (product isolation), analysis (sample preparation), quality control (particulate testing), and microbiology (sterile filtration).

## Common Mistakes
- Using wrong filter pore size (too coarse passes particles, too fine clogs)
- Forgetting to wet filter paper before vacuum filtration (poor seal, slow flow)
- Overfilling funnel above filter paper edge
- Applying vacuum before filter paper is seated (causes wrinkling, bypass)
- Using filter paper chemically incompatible with solvent (dissolves or weakens)

## Common Problems
- Very slow flow rate (clogged filter, too fine pore size, too much solid)
- Filter paper tearing (too much vacuum, sharp particles)
- Filtrate still cloudy (filter not seated, too coarse pore size, channeling)
- Vacuum not holding (poor seal, leak in apparatus)
- Filter cake cracking (allows liquid and particles through)

## Fixes
- Slow flow: pre-filter through coarse filter, reduce solid loading; use vacuum; switch to membrane or pressure filtration
- Tearing: reduce vacuum, use wet-strength filter paper, use support screen
- Cloudy filtrate: use finer filter, ensure proper seal, double filter
- Vacuum leak: check all connections, rubber adapter, stopcock; replace worn parts
- Cake cracking: keep cake covered with liquid until ready to dry

## Safety
- Wear gloves when handling filter papers with chemical residues
- Use fume hood for volatile solvent filtration
- Ensure vacuum flask is clamped/secured (can tip)
- Use trap flask between vacuum pump and filter to prevent backflow
- Sintered glass filters are fragile - handle with care
- Never filter strong oxidizing agents through paper filters (fire hazard)
- For biological samples: filter sterilize using 0.22 µm membrane in biosafety cabinet
