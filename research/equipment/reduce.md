# Reduce - Chemistry Lab Operation

## Purpose
To cause a substance to gain electrons (decrease oxidation state) through reaction with a reducing agent. Reduction is fundamental to organic synthesis (carbonyl→alcohol, nitro→amine), metal extraction from ores, and analytical chemistry.

## How It Works
A reducing agent (reductant) donates electrons to the substrate. Common reducing agents include LiAlH₄, NaBH₄, H₂ with metal catalyst (Pd/C, Pt, Raney Ni), metals (Na, Zn, Fe in acid), hydride donors, and electrochemical reduction. Catalytic hydrogenation adds H₂ across unsaturated bonds.

## Setup
- Fume hood (many reducing agents generate H₂ gas or toxic fumes)
- Dry glassware and anhydrous conditions for hydride reductions (LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water)
- Reaction vessel: round-bottom flask with septum for air-sensitive work
- Inert gas line (N₂/Ar) for air-sensitive reductions
- Addition funnel or syringe pump for controlled addition
- Temperature control: ice bath for exothermic, heating for slow reactions
- For hydrogenation: Parr hydrogenation apparatus (pressure vessel + H₂ cylinder + gauge)

## Usage
1. Dissolve substrate in anhydrous solvent (THF, ether for LiAlH₄; ethanol for NaBH₄)
2. Inert atmosphere: purge reaction vessel with N₂/Ar
3. Add reducing agent slowly (portionwise for solids, dropwise for solutions)
4. Control temperature - many reductions are exothermic
5. Monitor reaction progress by TLC or GC
6. When complete: quench excess reducing agent carefully (LiAlH₄: add water dropwise, then NaOH, then water; NaBH₄: add water or dilute acid)
7. Extract product, wash, dry over MgSO₄, filter, evaporate solvent
8. Purify if needed (chromatography, distillation)

## Results
Product identity confirmed by NMR, IR, MS. Yield calculated. Disappearance of characteristic peaks (e.g., carbonyl C=O in IR) confirms reduction.

## Completion
TLC shows no starting material, GC shows constant product composition, or H₂ uptake stops (in hydrogenation).

## Models
- Hydride reduction (LiAlH₄, NaBH₄, DIBAL-H) - most common for carbonyls
- Catalytic hydrogenation (H₂ + Pd/C, PtO₂, Raney Ni) - clean, scalable
- Metal reduction (Zn/HCl, Fe/HCl, Na/liq NH₃) - for nitro groups
- Electrochemical reduction - applied potential, no chemical waste
- Enzymatic reduction (oxidoreductases) - selective, green

## Display / Memory / USB / Connectivity / Voice / Buttons
Standard manual reduction: none. Parr apparatus: pressure gauge (analog or digital). Automated systems: LCD, USB, Ethernet for data logging.

## Ventilation
Required. Reductions can release H₂ (explosive), metal hydride fumes, or acidic gases. Use fume hood. For hydrogenation: good ventilation required to prevent H₂ accumulation.

## Limitations
- LiAlH₄ reacts violently with water and protic solvents
- Catalytic hydrogenation requires flammable H₂ gas under pressure
- Selectivity can be an issue (over-reduction)
- Some reductions require high temperature or pressure
- Metal reductions produce stoichiometric metal waste

## Cleaning
- Quench any residual reducing agent before cleaning glassware
- LiAlH₄ residues: react with ethyl acetate or isopropanol, then water
- NaBH₄ residues: rinse with dilute acid or water
- Glassware: standard lab detergent wash after decontamination
- Hydrogenation catalyst: collect and recycle (Pd is expensive)

## Storage
- LiAlH₄: store under N₂/Ar, in sealed container, away from water
- NaBH₄: store in sealed container, dry place
- H₂ cylinders: secure upright, store in ventilated area
- Metal catalysts: store under N₂, away from heat sources

## Disposal
- Quenched reducing agents: aqueous waste stream (after verification of complete reaction)
- Spent hydrogenation catalyst: collect for precious metal recycling
- LiAlH₄ waste: slow quench with ethyl acetate, then water, then base
- Follow institutional hazardous waste protocols

## Safety
- LiAlH₄ can ignite on contact with air or water - use extreme caution
- Catalytic hydrogenation: H₂ is flammable/explosive; use spark-free equipment
- Use blast shield for high-pressure hydrogenation
- Wear appropriate PPE (lab coat, safety glasses, flame-resistant gloves for hydrides)
- Have appropriate fire extinguisher (Class D for metal hydride fires)
- Know quenching procedures before starting reaction
- Never add water directly to LiAlH₄
- Vent H₂ gas safely away from ignition sources
