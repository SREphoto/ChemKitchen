# Heat - Chemistry Lab Operation

## Purpose
To increase the temperature of a substance or reaction mixture. Heating is used to accelerate reactions (following Arrhenius equation), dissolve solutes, promote evaporation, facilitate distillation, and activate chemical processes.

## How It Works
Thermal energy is transferred to the sample via conduction (hot plate, heating mantle), convection (hot air, oil bath), or radiation (IR lamp, microwave). The temperature rise increases molecular kinetic energy, overcoming activation energy barriers for reactions. Heating rate and temperature control are critical for reproducibility.

## Setup
- Heat source: hot plate, heating mantle, oil bath, sand bath, Bunsen burner, or microwave reactor
- Temperature measurement: thermometer, thermocouple, or IR sensor
- Temperature control: thermostat, variable transformer, or programmable controller
- Stirring: magnetic stirrer or overhead stirrer for even heat distribution
- Vessel: appropriate for temperature range (borosilicate glass to 500°C, quartz to 1200°C, metal for higher)
- Safety: fume hood, heat-resistant gloves, tongs

## Usage
1. Place vessel containing sample on/over heat source
2. Insert thermometer or thermocouple into sample or heating medium
3. Set desired temperature on controller (or adjust gas/voltage manually)
4. Begin stirring to ensure even heat distribution (prevent hot spots)
5. Monitor temperature throughout - do not exceed safe limits for vessel, contents, and equipment
6. When target temperature is reached, maintain as needed
7. After heating: turn off heat source, remove vessel carefully with tongs or heat-resistant gloves
8. Allow to cool before handling

## Results
Elevation to target temperature. Controlled heating achieves reproducible reaction rates, complete dissolution, or successful distillation.

## Completion
When target temperature is reached and maintained. For reactions: when heating period is complete per protocol.

## Models
- Hot plate: flat surface, direct contact heating. Simple, cheap, but temperature uneven without stirring.
- Heating mantle: hemispherical or cylindrical, surrounds flask. Even heating, fits round-bottom flasks. Various sizes available.
- Oil bath: vessel containing oil, flask immersed. Even heating, good temperature stability. Messy, limited temperature range by oil type.
- Sand bath: aluminum block or sand-filled tray. Dry, no oil mess. Less even than oil bath.
- Microwave reactor: rapid, selective heating. Expensive ($20,000+), specialized use.
- Heat gun: forced hot air. Quick heating of localized areas.
- Ovens/furnaces: for drying, high-temperature reactions, ashing.

## Display / Memory / USB / Connectivity / Voice / Buttons
- Basic: analog dial temperature control, no display
- Digital: LED/LCD showing actual and set temperature, stirring speed, timer
- Advanced: programmable temperature ramping, data logging via USB, remote monitoring (Ethernet/WiFi)
- Buttons: power, temperature set, heating on/off, stirrer control, timer

## Ventilation
Required when heating volatile, toxic, or flammable materials. Use fume hood. Bunsen burners consume O₂ and produce CO₂, CO - require good ventilation.

## Limitations
- Maximum temperature limited by vessel material and heat source
- Some materials decompose before melting
- Uneven heating can cause hot spots and localized decomposition
- Thermal shock can crack glassware
- Temperature overshoot with manual control
- Oil bath has upper temperature limit (silicone oil: ~250°C, mineral oil: ~180°C)

## Cleaning
- Hot plate: wipe cool surface with damp cloth; do not immerse
- Heating mantle: wipe exterior, do not immerse; if spill inside, use compressed air
- Oil bath: filter oil through paper filter to remove particles; replace when darkened
- Sand bath: discard contaminated sand, refill

## Storage
- Hot plates: store clean, cool, dry; coil cord neatly
- Heating mantles: store flat or hanging, not folded (wire damage risk)
- Oil bath: cover to prevent dust contamination
- Bunsen burners: disconnect gas hose, store in dry area

## Disposal
- Hot plate/heating mantle: electronic waste disposal
- Bunsen burner: metal recycling
- Used bath oil: chemical waste disposal (may contain chemical contamination)

## Lab Usage
Ubiquitous in chemistry labs. Used for reactions (reflux, synthesis), sample preparation (dissolution, digestion), analytical procedures (ashing, drying), and physical property measurements (melting point, boiling point).

## Common Mistakes
- Heating too rapidly (causes bumping, decomposition, thermal shock)
- Not using stirring (causes hot spots, uneven reaction)
- Heating sealed vessels (pressure buildup causes explosion)
- Using wrong vessel for temperature (glass shatters, PTFE decomposes above 260°C)
- Setting temperature above flash point of solvent
- Leaving heating unattended without automatic shutoff

## Common Problems
- Temperature overshoot (especially with manual control)
- Uneven heating (localized charring, decomposition)
- Bumping (sudden violent boiling, loss of product)
- Stirrer failure (magnetic stirrer loses coupling at high temperature)
- Oil bath smoking (oil above recommended temperature)
- Hot plate fails to reach temperature (element burned out)

## Fixes
- Overshoot: use PID controller, lower heating rate, use oil bath for thermal mass
- Uneven: increase stirring rate, use heating mantle that conforms to vessel shape
- Bumping: add boiling chips, use stirrer, heat gently, use anti-bumping granules
- Stirrer failure: use overhead stirrer for viscous mixtures, larger stir bar
- Oil smoking: use oil with higher flash point, lower temperature
- Burned out element: replace hot plate, check electrical connection

## Safety
- Use heat-resistant gloves when handling hot vessels
- Never leave heating unattended without monitoring
- Use fume hood for heating volatile or toxic substances
- Keep flammable materials away from heat sources
- Do not heat sealed vessels - risk of explosion
- Use boiling chips to prevent bumping
- Allow equipment to cool before cleaning
- Have fire extinguisher nearby (Class B for flammable liquids, Class C for electrical)
- Bunsen burner: tie back hair, loose clothing; check gas connections for leaks
- Position hot plate away from edge of bench to prevent accidental knocking
- For high-temperature work (>300°C): use appropriate PPE including face shield
