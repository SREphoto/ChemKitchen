# Synthesize - Chemistry Lab Operation

## Purpose
To produce a desired chemical compound from simpler starting materials (precursors) via one or more chemical reactions. Synthesis is the fundamental operation of preparative chemistry, creating everything from simple salts to complex pharmaceutical molecules.

## How It Works
Selected reactants are combined under controlled conditions (temperature, pressure, solvent, catalyst, time) to undergo chemical transformation. Reaction mechanisms vary (e.g., nucleophilic substitution, condensation, Grignard, Heck coupling). Bonds are broken and formed according to thermodynamic and kinetic principles, yielding the target product which is then isolated and purified.

## Setup
- Requires a fume hood or well-ventilated area
- Assemble glassware: round-bottom flask, condenser, addition funnel, magnetic stirrer
- Heating mantle or oil bath for temperature control
- Thermometer or thermocouple for monitoring
- Inert gas line (N₂/Ar) if moisture/air-sensitive
- Weigh reactants on analytical balance
- Set up reflux or distillation apparatus as needed

## Usage
1. Add solvent and reactants to flask in correct order (often slowly to control exotherms)
2. Apply heat and stirring as required
3. Monitor reaction progress via TLC, GC, HPLC, or NMR sampling
4. When complete, cool the reaction mixture
5. Quench if necessary (careful addition of water, acid, or base)
6. Extract product into organic layer using separatory funnel
7. Wash organic layer with brine, dry over MgSO₄ or Na₂SO₄
8. Filter and evaporate solvent using rotary evaporator
9. Purify: column chromatography, recrystallization, or distillation
10. Characterize: melting point, NMR, IR, mass spectrometry

## Results
Isolated yield (mass and percentage) and purity assessment via analytical methods. Yield calculated as (actual mass / theoretical mass) × 100%. Purity confirmed by melting point range, TLC single spot, HPLC purity, or NMR spectrum.

## Completion
Reaction is complete when TLC shows no starting material remaining, GC/HPLC shows constant composition, or characteristic color change/ gas evolution ceases. Typical times: minutes to days depending on reaction.

## Ventilation
Required. All synthesis must be performed in a fume hood or under ventilation canopy to prevent exposure to hazardous vapors, gases, and aerosols.

## Limitations
- Scale: mg to kg depending on equipment
- Some reactions require high pressure, inert atmosphere, or cryogenic conditions
- Time-intensive setup and workup
- Yield limited by side reactions and equilibrium
- Cannot synthesize all theoretically possible compounds (kinetic/thermodynamic barriers)

## Cleaning
- Glassware: rinse first with appropriate solvent, then wash with lab detergent, rinse with distilled water, oven-dry
- Remove all traces of reactants/products promptly
- Stubborn residues: aqua regia (organics), base bath (silicates), piranha solution (trace metals) - extreme caution required
- PTFE components: soak in solvent, sonicate if needed

## Storage
- Store reagents by compatibility class (oxidizers separate from flammables)
- Store products in labeled containers with hazard and date information
- Air/moisture-sensitive: desiccators or glove boxes
- Light-sensitive: amber glass or foil-wrapped containers

## Disposal
- All chemical waste per institutional/regulatory guidelines
- Separate organic, aqueous, halogenated, solid waste streams
- Heavy metal waste: special disposal required
- Consult SDS for specific disposal methods

## Lab Usage
Core operation of preparative chemistry. Used across organic, inorganic, organometallic, polymer, and materials chemistry.

## Common Mistakes
- Incorrect stoichiometry (not accounting for purity or hydration)
- Adding reagents too quickly causing exotherm
- Insufficient drying of glassware for moisture-sensitive reactions
- Overheating leading to decomposition
- Not monitoring reaction progress sufficiently

## Common Problems
- Low yield (side reactions, incomplete conversion, losses during workup)
- Impure product (inadequate purification or decomposition)
- Reaction fails to proceed (old reagents, wrong conditions)
- Exothermic runaway (especially at scale)
- Emulsion formation during extraction

## Fixes
- Low yield: optimize stoichiometry, time, temperature; improve workup
- Impure product: recrystallize, chromatography, redistill
- Failed reaction: check reagent freshness, add catalyst, troubleshoot conditions
- Exotherm: slower addition, improved cooling, larger vessel
- Emulsion: add brine, centrifuge, let stand, add small amount of ethanol

## Safety
- Always wear PPE: lab coat, safety glasses, chemical-resistant gloves
- Perform all synthesis in a fume hood
- Know hazards of all reagents (consult SDS beforehand)
- Have spill kit and appropriate fire extinguisher available
- Never leave active reactions unattended without monitoring
- Prepare for exotherms, gas evolution, pressure buildup
- Quench reactions carefully (especially reactive metals, hydrides, acid chlorides)
- Label all vessels with contents, hazards, and date
